abs: 'A rigid body can fit at its start and goal yet collide with a wall as it moves between them. Inspired by the moving sofa problem, we introduce SofaSpace, a benchmark for visual spatial reasoning through continuous planar rigid-body transport and environment construction. SofaSpace contains 3,000 scenes organized around three challenges: geometric compatibility, nonlocal maneuver planning, and interactive and temporal coordination. Four task views ask models to plan a motion, reconstruct a layout, design an environment that meets transport requirements, or jointly generate a layout and a motion. A separate set of 534 SofaSpace-Hard tasks tests planning and judgment. A geometric verifier checks the entire body throughout each submitted motion. On static planning tasks, endpoint agreement and continuous validity differ substantially within the same submitted plans: Claude drops from 97.2% to 52.8%, DeepSeek from 100.0% to 30.6%, and Kimi from 97.2% to 29.4%, while the strongest configuration retains 98.3% full validity. Environment construction reveals a further gap. Of 295 feasible layouts generated by the strongest configuration, 181 satisfy all evaluated functional requirements; for Kimi, one of 172 does. These results show how executable checks distinguish endpoint agreement from valid motion and geometric feasibility from functional compliance.'
